## Supplied solution 1

**First solution (authors)** By Cauchy-Schwarz, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
\sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{k+1}{k^3} &= \sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{\left(\frac{1}{k^2}\right)^2}{\frac{1}{k(k+1)}} \\
&\ge \frac{\left(\frac{1}{m^2} + \frac{1}{(m+1)^2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n^2}\right)^2}{\frac{1}{m(m+1)} + \frac{1}{(m+1)(m+2)} + \dots + \frac{1}{n(n+1)}} \\
&= \frac{\left(\frac{1}{m^2} + \frac{1}{(m+1)^2} + \dots + \frac{1}{n^2}\right)^2}{\frac{1}{m} - \frac{1}{n+1}} \\
&> \frac{\left(\sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{1}{k^2}\right)^2}{\frac{1}{m}}
\end{align*}
$$
as desired.


**Second approach by inducting down, Luke Robitaille and Carl Schildkraut** Fix $n$; we'll induct downwards on $m$. For the base case of $n = m$ the result is easy, since the left side is $\frac{m+1}{m^3}$ and the right side is $\frac{m}{m^4} = \frac{1}{m^3}$.
For the inductive step, suppose we have shown the result for $m+1$. Let

$$
A = \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} \frac{1}{k^2} \quad \text{and} \quad B = \sum_{k=m+1}^{n} \frac{1}{k^3}.
$$
We know $A + B \ge (m+1)A^2$, and we want to show
$$
\left(A + \frac{1}{m^2}\right) + \left(B + \frac{1}{m^3}\right) \ge m \left(A + \frac{1}{m^2}\right)^2.
$$

$$
\left(A + \frac{1}{m^2}\right) + \left(B + \frac{1}{m^3}\right) - m \left(A + \frac{1}{m^2}\right)^2 = A + B + \frac{m+1}{m^3} - mA^2 - \frac{2A}{m} - \frac{1}{m^3} \\
= \left(A + B - (m+1)A^2\right) + \left(A - \frac{1}{m}\right)^2 \ge 0,
$$
and we are done.


**Third approach by reducing $n \to \infty$, Michael Ren and Carl Schildkraut** First, we give:

Set $n = \infty$ henceforth. Let
$$
A = \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2} \quad \text{and} \quad B = \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^3};
$$

Write
$$
C = \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} \frac{1}{k^2(2k-1)(2k+1)} \quad \text{and} \quad D = \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} \frac{8k^2 - 1}{k^3(2k-1)^2(2k+1)^2}.
$$

$$
\frac{2}{2k-1} - \frac{2}{2k+1} = \frac{1}{k^2} + \frac{1}{k^2(2k-1)(2k+1)},
$$
and
$$
\frac{2}{(2k-1)^2} - \frac{2}{(2k+1)^2} = \frac{1}{k^3} + \frac{8k^2-1}{k^3(2k-1)^2(2k+1)^2},
$$

$$
A = \frac{2}{2m-1} - C \quad \text{and} \quad B = \frac{2}{(2m-1)^2} - D.
$$

$$
\frac{2m+1}{2m-1}C \ge D + mC^2.
$$

**Claim** — We have
$$
\frac{2m + 1/2}{2m - 1} C \geq D.
$$

*Proof.* We compare termwise; we need
$$
\frac{2m + 1/2}{2m - 1} \cdot \frac{1}{k^2(2k - 1)(2k + 1)} \ge \frac{8k^2 - 1}{k^3(2k - 1)^2(2k + 1)^2}
$$
for $k \ge m$. It suffices to show
$$
\frac{2k + 1/2}{2k - 1} \cdot \frac{1}{k^2(2k - 1)(2k + 1)} \ge \frac{8k^2 - 1}{k^3(2k - 1)^2(2k + 1)^2},
$$
which is equivalent to $k(2k + 1/2)(2k + 1) \ge 8k^2 - 1$. This holds for all $k \ge 1$. $\square$

**Claim** — We have
$$
\frac{1/2}{2m-1}C \ge mC^2.
$$

*Proof.* We need $C \le 1/(2m(2m-1))$; indeed,
$$
\frac{1}{2m(2m-1)} = \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} \left( \frac{1}{2k(2k-1)} - \frac{1}{2(k+1)(2k+1)} \right) = \sum_{k=m}^{\infty} \frac{4k+1}{2k(2k-1)(k+1)(2k+1)};
$$
comparing term-wise with the definition of $C$ and using the inequality $k(4k+1) \ge 2(k+1)$ for $k \ge 1$ gives the desired result. $\square$


**Fourth approach by bashing, Carl Schildkraut** With a bit more work, the third approach can be adapted to avoid the $n \to \infty$ reduction. Similarly to before, define
$$
A = \sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{1}{k^2} \quad \text{and} \quad B = \sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{1}{k^3};
$$
we want to show $1 + 4mB \ge (2mA - 1)^2$. Writing

$$
C = \sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{1}{k^2(2k-1)(2k+1)} \quad \text{and} \quad D = \sum_{k=m}^{n} \frac{8k^2 - 1}{k^3(2k-1)^2(2k+1)^2}.
$$
We compute
$$
A = \frac{2}{2m-1} - \frac{2}{2n+1} - C \quad \text{and} \quad B = \frac{2}{(2m-1)^2} - \frac{2}{(2n+1)^2} - D.
$$

Then, the inequality we wish to show reduces (as in the previous solution) to
$$
\frac{2m+1}{2m-1}C + \frac{2(2m+1)}{(2m-1)(2n+1)} \ge D + mC^2 + \frac{2(2m+1)}{(2n+1)^2} + \frac{4m}{2n+1}C.
$$

We deal first with the terms not containing the variable $n$, i.e. we show that
$$
\frac{2m+1}{2m-1}C \ge D + mC^2.
$$

For this part, the two claims from the previous solution go through exactly as written above, and we have $C \le 1/(2m(2m-1))$. We now need to show
$$
\frac{2(2m+1)}{(2m-1)(2n+1)} \ge \frac{2(2m+1)}{(2n+1)^2} + \frac{4m}{2n+1}C
$$
(this is just the inequality between the remaining terms); our bound on $C$ reduces this to proving
$$
\frac{4(2m+1)(n-m+1)}{(2m-1)(2n+1)^2} \ge \frac{2}{(2m-1)(2n+1)}.
$$
Expanding and writing in terms of $n$, this is equivalent to
$$
n \ge \frac{1 + 2(m - 1)(2m + 1)}{4m} = m - \frac{2m + 1}{4m},
$$
which holds for all $n \ge m$.
