## Supplied solution 1

The answer is no. It is enough to show that if $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i \le n^2$ for any $n$, then $\sum_{i=2}^{2n} \frac{1}{a_i} > \frac{n}{4}$. (or any other precise estimate)

For this, we use that $\sum_{i=2^k+1}^{2^{k+1}} a_i \le \sum_{i=2^k+1}^{2^{k+1}} \frac{1}{a_i} \ge 2^{2k}$ for any $k \ge 0$ by the arithmetic-harmonic mean inequality.

Since $\sum_{i=2^k+1}^{2^{k+1}} a_i < \sum_{i=1}^{2^{k+1}} a_i \le 2^{2k+2}$, it follows that $\sum_{i=2^k+1}^{2^{k+1}} \frac{1}{a_i} > \frac{1}{4}$ and hence

$$
\sum_{i=2}^{2n} \frac{1}{a_i} > \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \sum_{i=2^k+1}^{2^{k+1}} \frac{1}{a_i} > \frac{n}{4}.
$$
