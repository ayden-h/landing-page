## Supplied solution 1

First we show that such a function should be injective. Indeed, if $f(a) = f(b)$ then $\forall y > 0$
$$
\frac{f(yf(a))}{f(2yf(a) + f(y))} = \frac{f(yf(b))}{f(2yf(b) + f(y))}
$$
and by the given relation
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{a+y}{a^3} &= \frac{b+y}{b^3} \\
& \Leftrightarrow b^3(a+y) = a^3(b+y) \\
& \Leftrightarrow (b-a)(a^2b + a^2y + ab^2 + ab y + b^2 y) = 0 \\
& \Leftrightarrow a = b, \quad \text{since } a^2b + a^2y + ab^2 + ab y + b^2 y > 0.
\end{align*}
$$
Now notice that for $x > 1$ it is $x^3 - x > 0$, and setting $y = x^3 - x$ in the relation we get
$$
f(2(x^3 - x)f(x) + f(x^3 - x)) = f((x^3 - x)f(x)), \quad \forall x > 1.
$$
Injectivity of $f$ then implies
$$
\begin{align*}
& 2(x^3 - x)f(x) + f(x^3 - x) = (x^3 - x)f(x), \quad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}^+ \\
\Leftrightarrow & f(x^3 - x) = (x - x^3)f(x), \quad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}^+.
\end{align*}
$$
The last relation for $x = 2$ gives
$$
f(6) = f(2^3 - 2) = (2 - 2^3)f(2) = -6f(2) < 0,
$$
which is absurd. So there exists no such function $f$.
