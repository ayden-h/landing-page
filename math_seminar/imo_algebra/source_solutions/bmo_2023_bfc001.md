## Supplied solution 1

Answer: There exist such $P$ and $Q$.
Let $M = 2023!$ and $0 < a_1 < a_2 < \dots < a_{2024} < 1$. We will show that the polynomials $P(x) = (x - a_1) \cdot (x - a_2) \cdots (x - a_{2024}) + M$ and $Q(x) = (x - (M+1)) \cdot (x - (M+2)) \cdots (x - (M + 2023)) + M$ fulfill the stated conditions.

For $x \in [0, 1]$, $P$ does not have a real root since $|(x - a_1) \cdots (x - a_{2024})| < 1$. It is clear that $P(x) > 0$ when $x \notin [0, 1]$, so $P$ has no any real root.

In the interval $(-\infty, M+1)$ the function $Q(x)$ is increasing and can have at most one real root. Obviously, $Q(M) = 0$. For $x \in [M+1, M+2023]$ we can easily show that $|(x - (M+1)) \cdot (x - (M+2)) \cdots (x - (M + 2023))| < M$, so $Q$ does not have real root in that interval. As it is obvious that $Q(x) > 0$ for $x > M + 2023$, the only real root of the polynomial $Q$ is equal to $M$.

Therefore
$$
|\{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid P(P(x)) \cdot Q(Q(x)) = 0\}| = |\{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid Q(x) = M\}|
$$
$$
= |\{M+1, \dots, M+2023\}| = 2023 \text{ and}
$$
$$
|\{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid P(Q(x)) \cdot Q(P(x)) = 0\}| = |\{x \in \mathbb{R} \mid P(x) = M\}| = |\{a_1, \dots, a_{2024}\}| = 2024.
$$

**Remark.** The example can be constructed in various ways. The main idea is to choose $P$ with no real roots and $Q$ with a single real root.
