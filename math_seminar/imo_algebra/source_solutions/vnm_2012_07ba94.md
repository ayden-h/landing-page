## Supplied solution 1

Denote by $\alpha$ and $\beta$ the common differences of the arithmetic progressions $a_n$ and $b_n$, respectively. The quadratics $P_k$ ($k = 1, 2, \dots, m$) can be written as
$$
P_k(x) = x^2 + (a_1 + (k-1)\alpha)x + b_1 + (k-1)\beta, \quad k = 1, 2, \dots, m.
$$
Since $P_1$ and $P_m$ have no real root, we have
$$
\Delta_1 = a_1^2 - 4b_1 < 0
$$
and
$$
\Delta_m = (a_1 + (m-1)\alpha)^2 - 4(b_1 + (m-1)\beta) < 0.
$$
Suppose that there exists $k \le m$ such that the quadratic $P_k$ has a real root. We have
$$
\Delta_k = (a_1 + (k-1)\alpha)^2 - 4(b_1 + (k-1)\beta) \ge 0.
$$
Since $k, m > 1$, it follows from the above and by the previous inequalities that
$$
(k-1)\Delta_m < 0 < (m-1)\Delta_k,
$$
which is equivalent to
$$
(k-1)a_1^2 + (k-1)(m-1)^2\alpha^2 - 4(k-1)b_1 < (m-1)a_1^2 + (m-1)(k-1)^2\alpha^2 - 4(m-1)b_1.
$$
This implies that
$$
\begin{align*}
(k-1)(m-1)(m-k)^2\alpha^2 &< (m-k)a_1^2 - 4(m-k)b_1 \\
(k-1)(m-1)^2\alpha^2 &< a_1^2 - 4b_1 \quad (\text{since } m > k) \\
\Rightarrow a_1^2 - 4b_1 &\ge 0,
\end{align*}
$$
which contradicts $\Delta_1 = a_1^2 - 4b_1 < 0$. This completes our proof.
