## Supplied solution 1

(1) Let $a, b$ be positive integers. Since
$$
\begin{aligned}
(a+b)(f(a)+f(b)) &= (a+b)f(a) + (a+b)f(b) \\
&\le f(a(a+b)) + f(b(a+b)) \\
&\le af(a+b) + a-1 + bf(a+b) + b-1 \\
&< (a+b)(f(a+b)+1),
\end{aligned}
$$
we have $f(a) + f(b) < f(a + b) + 1$. Because $f$ is an integer-valued function, $f(a) + f(b) \le f(a + b)$. Similarly, since
$$
\begin{aligned}
(a+b)f(a+b) &= af(a+b) + bf(a+b) \\
&\le f(a(a+b)) + f(b(a+b)) \\
&\le (a+b)f(a) + (a+b)f(b) + 2(a+b) - 2 \\
&< (a+b)(f(a) + f(b) + 2),
\end{aligned}
$$
we have $f(a+b) < f(a)+f(b)+2$ and hence $f(a+b) \le f(a)+f(b)+1$.

**Alternative 1.** For a real number $x$, let $\lfloor x \rfloor$ denote the largest integer less than or equal to $x$. By the given condition,
$$
\frac{f(n)}{n} \le \frac{f(kn)}{kn} \le \frac{f(n)}{n} + \frac{k-1}{kn} < \frac{f(n)}{n} + 1.
$$
Therefore, for any positive integers $k$ and $n$, we obtain
$$
f(n) = \left\lfloor \frac{f(kn)}{kn} \right\rfloor
$$
Using the above formula, we can prove the desired inequalities as follows:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(a+b) &= \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{ab} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{b(a+b)} + \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{a(a+b)} \right\rfloor \\
&\ge \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{b(a+b)} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{a(a+b)} \right\rfloor = f(a) + f(b);
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(a+b) &= \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{ab} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{b(a+b)} + \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{a(a+b)} \right\rfloor \\
&\le \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{b(a+b)} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{f(ab(a+b))}{a(a+b)} \right\rfloor + 1 = f(a) + f(b) + 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Alternative 2.** By the given condition, we have
$$
k f(n) \le f(kn) = f(nk) \le n f(k) + n - 1.
$$
By substituting $(k, n)$ with $(a+b, a)$ and $(a+b, b)$, we have
$$
(a+b)f(a) \le af(a+b) + a - 1 \quad \text{and} \quad (a+b)f(b) \le bf(a+b) + b - 1,
$$
respectively. From the above inequalities, we have
$$
(a+b)(f(a)+f(b)) \le (a+b)f(a+b) + (a+b) - 2 \\ < (a+b)(f(a+b)+1),
$$
and hence $f(a) + f(b) \le f(a+b)$.
Similarly, by substituting $(k, n)$ with $(a, a+b)$ and $(b, a+b)$, we have
$$
af(a+b) \le (a+b)f(a) + a+b-1
$$
and
$$
bf(a+b) \le (a+b)f(b) + a+b-1,
$$
respectively. From the above inequalities, we have
$$
(a+b)(f(a+b)) \le (a+b)(f(a)+f(b)) + 2(a+b) - 2 \\ < (a+b)(f(a)+f(b)+2)
$$
and hence $f(a+b) \le f(a) + f(b) + 1$.

(2) Consider the following set
$$
S = \{f(2007n) - 2007f(n) \mid n = 1, \dots, 2007\}.
$$
Because $2007f(n) \le f(2007n) \le 2007f(n) + 2005$, the set $S$ has at most 2006 elements. By the pigeonhole principle, there exist distinct positive integers $k, m \le 2007$ such that
$$
f(2007k) - 2007f(k) = f(2007m) - 2007f(m),
$$
hence
$$
f(2007k) - f(2007m) = 2007(f(k) - f(m)).
$$
Without loss of generality, we may assume $k > m$. Let $k = m + c$. From the above equation, we have
$$
f(2007(m+c)) - f(2007m) = 2007(f(m+c) - f(m)). \quad \dots\dots (*)
$$
Note that, by the given condition in (1),
$$
\begin{cases} f(2007c) \le f(2007(m+c)) - f(2007m) \le f(2007c) + 1; \\ f(c) \le f(m+c) - f(m) \le f(c) + 1. \end{cases}
$$

\text{Case 1} \left\{ \begin{array}{l} f(2007(m+c)) - f(2007m) = f(2007c); \\ f(m+c) - f(m) = f(c) \end{array} \right.
$$
By (*), we have $f(2007c) = 2007f(c)$ as desired.
$$
\text{Case 2} \left\{ \begin{array}{l} f(2007(m+c)) - f(2007m) = f(2007c); \\ f(m+c) - f(m) = f(c) + 1 \end{array} \right.
$$
By (*), we have $f(2007c) = 2007f(c) + 2007$. This contradicts the
condition $f(2007n) \le 2007f(n) + 2005$.
$$
\text{Case 3} \left\{ \begin{array}{l} f(2007(m+c)) - f(2007m) = f(2007c) + 1; \\ f(m+c) - f(m) = f(c) \end{array} \right.
$$
By (*), we have $f(2007c)+1 = 2007f(c)$. This contradicts the condition
that $kf(n) \le f(kn)$.
$$
\text{Case 4} \left\{ \begin{array}{l} f(2007(m+c)) - f(2007m) = f(2007c) + 1; \\ f(m+c) - f(m) = f(c) + 1 \end{array} \right.
$$
By (*), we have $f(2007c) = 2007f(c) + 2006$. This contradicts the
condition $f(2007n) \le 2007f(n) + 2005$. $\square$
