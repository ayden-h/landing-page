## Supplied solution 1

Without loss of generality, we assume that $s > t$. Then we have that
$$
a_s - a_t = a^t + a^{t+1} + \dots + a^{s-1} = a^t a_{s-t}.
$$
So, in order to prove that $s-t|a_s - a_t$, it is enough to prove that $s-t|a_{s-t}$, or equivalently that for every prime power $p^k|s-t$, then $p^k|a_{s-t}$. We write $s-t = m$ and $m = p^k r$, where $r$ is a positive integer and $b = a^r$. Then,
$$
\begin{aligned}
1 + a + \dots + a^{m-1} &= \frac{a^m - 1}{a-1} = \frac{a^r - 1}{a-1} \cdot \frac{a^m - 1}{a^r - 1} = \frac{a^r - 1}{a-1} \cdot \frac{a^{p^k r} - 1}{a^r - 1} \\
&= \frac{a^r - 1}{a-1} \cdot \frac{b^{p^k} - 1}{b-1} = \frac{a^r - 1}{a-1} \prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{b^{p^i} - 1}{b^{p^{i-1}} - 1} \\
&= \frac{a^r - 1}{a-1} \prod_{i=1}^{k} \left( 1 + b^{p^{i-1}} + b^{2p^{i-1}} + \dots + b^{(p-1)p^{i-1}} \right).
\end{aligned}
$$
We now observe that each of the terms in the above product is divisible by $p$. Indeed, since $b = a^r \equiv 1 \pmod{p}$ we have that
$$
1 + b^{p^i-1} + b^{2p^i-1} + \dots + b^{(p-1)p^i-1} \equiv 1 + 1 + \dots + 1 = p \equiv 0 \pmod{p},
$$
for each $1 \le i \le p$. We conclude that $1 + a + \dots + a^{m-1} = a_{s-t}$ is divisible by $p^k$. $\square$
