## Supplied solution 1

$[1 + \sqrt{2}]$
Let us denote by *S* and *T* the numerator and the denominator, respectively of the fraction to be computed. Namely,
$$
S = \sum_{n=1}^{99} (\sqrt{10 + \sqrt{n}}), \quad T = \sum_{n=1}^{99} (\sqrt{10 - \sqrt{n}}).
$$
First, we note that for a pair of positive real numbers *a*, *b*, we have
$$
\sqrt{a+b+2\sqrt{ab}} = \sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}.
$$
In particular, if we choose, for each fixed $n$ such that $1 \le n \le 99$, $a$ and $b$ in such a way that $a+b=20$ and $ab=n$ are satisfied, then we get
$$
\sqrt{20 + 2\sqrt{n}} = \sqrt{10 + \sqrt{100-n}} + \sqrt{10 - \sqrt{100-n}}
$$
Therefore, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned} \sqrt{2}S &= \sum_{n=1}^{99} \sqrt{20 + 2\sqrt{n}} = \sum_{n=1}^{99} \left( \sqrt{10 + \sqrt{100-n}} + \sqrt{10 - \sqrt{100-n}} \right) \\ &= \sum_{n=1}^{99} \left( \sqrt{10 + \sqrt{n}} + \sqrt{10 - \sqrt{n}} \right) = S + T, \end{aligned}
$$
which yields $(\sqrt{2} - 1)S = T$, and therefore, we get $\frac{S}{T} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-1} = 1 + \sqrt{2}$. as the desired solution to the problem.
