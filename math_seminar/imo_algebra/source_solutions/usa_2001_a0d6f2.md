## Supplied solution 1

**First Solution.** (By Tiankai Liu) Let
$$
f(k) = (n+1-k)!(n+k)!
$$
for integers $0 \le k \le n + 1$. Note that
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(k) + f(k + 1) &= (n+1-k)!(n+k)! + (n-k)!(n+k+1)! \\
&= (n + 1 - k + n + k + 1)(n-k)!(n+k)! \\
&= 2(n + 1)(n-k)!(n+k)!.
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k (n-k)!(n+k)! &= \frac{1}{2(n+1)} \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k [f(k) + f(k+1)] \\
&= \frac{f(0) + (-1)^n f(n+1)}{2(n+1)} \\
&= \frac{(n+1)!n! + (-1)^n 0!(2n+1)!}{2(n+1)} \\
&= \frac{(n!)^2}{2} + \frac{(-1)^n (2n+1)!}{2(n+1)}.
\end{aligned}
$$


**Second Solution.** (by Gabriel Carroll) Let
$$
f(x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} k!x^k \quad \text{and} \quad g(x) = f(x)f(-x).
$$
Note that
$$
\begin{align*}
f'(x) &= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+1)!(k+1)x^k \\
&= \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+2)!x^k - \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (k+1)!x^k \\
&= \frac{f(x) - x - 1}{x^2} - \frac{f(x) - 1}{x} \\
&= \frac{f(x)(1-x) - 1}{x^2}
\end{align*}
$$
and
$$
\begin{align*}
g'(x) &= f(-x)f'(x) - f(x)f'(-x) \\
&= \frac{f(-x)f(x)(1-x) - f(-x)}{x^2} - \frac{f(x)f(-x)(1+x) - f(x)}{x^2} \\
&= \frac{f(x) - f(-x)}{x^2} - \frac{2g(x)}{x}.
\end{align*}
$$
Denote by $[x^k]h(x)$ the coefficient of $x^k$ in a power series $h(x)$. We have
$$
\begin{align*}
[x^{2n}]g(x) &= \frac{1}{2n}[x^{2n-1}]g'(x) \\
&= \frac{1}{2n}[x^{2n-1}]\left(\frac{f(x)-f(-x)}{x^2} - \frac{2g(x)}{x}\right) \\
&= \frac{[x^{2n+1}](f(x)-f(-x))}{2n} - \frac{[x^{2n}]g(x)}{n}.
\end{align*}
$$
It follows that
$$
[x^{2n}]g(x) \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right) = \frac{2(2n+1)!}{2n},
$$
or
$$
[x^{2n}]g(x) = \frac{(2n+1)!}{n+1}. \qquad (1)
$$
---
On the other hand,
$$
\begin{aligned}
g(x) &= f(x)f(-x) = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} k!x^k \sum_{k=0}^{\infty} (-1)^k k!x^k \\
&= \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^{n-k} k!(n-k)! \right) x^n.
\end{aligned}
$$
It follows that
$$
\begin{aligned}
[x^{2n}]g(x) &= \sum_{k=0}^{2n} (-1)^{2n-k} k!(2n-k)! = \sum_{k=0}^{2n} (-1)^k k!(2n-k)! \\
&= 2 \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k k!(2n-k)! - (-1)^n n!^2 \\
&= 2 \sum_{m=0}^{n} (-1)^{n-m} (n-m)!(n+m)! - (-1)^n n!^2.
\end{aligned}
$$
The last step can be seen easily by taking $m = n - k$. Thus,
$$
[x^{2n}]g(x) = 2 \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^{n-k} (n-k)!(n+k)! - (-1)^n n!^2,
$$
or
$$
(-1)^n [x^{2n}] g(x) + n!^2 = 2 \sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^{-k} (n-k)!(n+k)! \quad (2)
$$
Combining (1) and (2) yields
$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{k=0}^{n} (-1)^k (n-k)!(n+k)! &= \frac{(-1)^n [x^{2n}] g(x) + (n!)^2}{2} \\
&= \frac{(-1)^n (2n+1)!}{2(n+1)} + \frac{(n!)^2}{2}.
\end{aligned}
$$
