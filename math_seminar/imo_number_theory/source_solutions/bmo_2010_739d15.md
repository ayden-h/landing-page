## Supplied solution 1

Write $x^3 = (1 + y\sqrt{-2})(1 - y\sqrt{-2})$. The identity
$$
(1 - y\sqrt{-2} - y^2)(1 + y\sqrt{-2}) - y^2(1 - y\sqrt{-2}) = 1
$$
shows that $1 + y\sqrt{-2}$ and $1 - y\sqrt{-2}$ are relatively prime in $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$. Since $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-2}]$ is a unique factorization domain,
$$
1 + y\sqrt{-2} = (u + v\sqrt{-2})^3 = u(u^2 - 6v^2) + v(3u^2 - 2v^2)\sqrt{-2},
$$
for some $u, v \in \mathbb{Z}$. Therefore, $u(u^2 - 6v^2) = 1$, which forces $u = 1$ and $v = 0$. Consequently, $y = 0$ and $x = 1$, which are obviously solutions to the given equation. $\square$
