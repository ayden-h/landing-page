## Supplied solution 1

It is easily seen that for $z = 1, 2, 3$ the given equation has no solution. Let $z \ge 4$. Then $2^x 3^y = (z-1)(z+1)$. Both of the numbers $z-1$ and $z+1$ can't be divisible by $3$ (because if they are then $3 | (z+1)-(z-1) = 2$, which is impossible). From $2 | (z-1)(z+1)$ it follows that the numbers $z-1$ and $z+1$ are divisible by $2$ and obviously only one of them can be divisible by $4$ (similarly as above). Hence we have the following cases:

a) $z+1 = 2 \cdot 3^y$, $z-1 = 2^{x-1}$

b) $z+1 = 2^{x-1}$, $z-1 = 2 \cdot 3^y$

Case a).
If we subtract the following equations: $z+1 = 2 \cdot 3^y$, $z-1 = 2^{x-1}$ we obtain $3^y - 2^{x-2} = 1$. It is easily seen that for $x = 2$ the last equation has no solution. For $x = 3$ we get $3^y = 1 + 2 = 3$ i.e. $y = 1$ and then $z = 5$. So one solution is the triple $(x, y, z) = (3, 1, 5)$. If $x \ge 4$, then $3^y \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$ hence $y$ is even. Let $y = 2y_1$, $y_1 \in \mathbb{N}$. By substitution in the equation $3^y - 2^{x-2} = 1$ we get $3^{2y_1} - 1 = 2^{x-2}$, i.e. $(3^{y_1} - 1)(3^{y_1} + 1) = 2^{x-2}$, from where we obtain that $3^{y_1} - 1 = 2$ and $3^{y_1} + 1 = 2^{x-3}$. Hence $y_1 = 1$, $y = 2$ and $x = 5$, $z = 17$. So the triple $(x, y, z) = (5, 2, 17)$ is a solution.

Case b).
$z+1 = 2^{x-1}$, $z-1 = 2 \cdot 3^y$. By subtraction we get $2^{x-1} - 2 \cdot 3^y = 2$ i.e. $2^{x-2} - 3^y = 1$. If $y = 1$, then $x = 4$ and $z = 7$, so $(x, y, z) = (4, 1, 7)$ is a solution. Let $y \ge 2$. $2^{x-2} \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$ i.e. $x-2$ is an even number, $x-2 = 2x_1$. With substitution in the equation $2^{x-2} - 3^y = 1$ we get $3^y = 2^{2x_1} - 1 = (2^{x_1} - 1)(2^{x_1} + 1)$, which implies that $2^{x_1} - 1 = 1$ or $2^{x_1} - 1 = 3$. If $2^{x_1} - 1 = 1$ we have that $x_1 = 1$, $x = 4$. Hence the triple $(4, 1, 7)$ is a solution. If $2^{x_1} - 1 = 3$ then $x_1 = 2$, i.e. $x = 6$ and we get that $3^y = 15$, which is impossible.

Finally, the solutions are
$$
(x, y, z) = (3, 1, 5),\ (5, 2, 17),\ (4, 1, 7).
$$
