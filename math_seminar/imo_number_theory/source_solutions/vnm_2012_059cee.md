## Supplied solution 1

From the given assumptions, we have
$$
(a, b) = 1, \text{ and} \tag{1a}
$$
$$
a^2 + b^2 + 2 \equiv 0 \pmod{ab}. \tag{2a}
$$
By (2a), there exists an integer $k$ such that $a^2 + b^2 + 2 = kab$. We will prove that $k = 4$.
If $a = b$, by (1a), we have $a = b = 1$. This implies that $k = 4$.
Consider the case $a \neq b$. Without loss of generality, we suppose that $a < b$. We consider the sequence defined by
$$
a_1 = b,\ a_2 = a, \text{ and } a_{i+2} = k a_{i+1} - a_i,\ \forall i \ge 2.
$$
We can easily prove the following properties of the sequence $(a_n)$:
1. All $a_n$ are odd positive integers. \hfill (p₁)
2. $(a_n)$ is a decreasing sequence. \hfill (p₂)
3. $\forall i \ge 2,\ a_{i-1}$ and $a_{i+1}$ are the roots of the equation (with variable $x$):
$$
x^2 - k a_i x + a_i^2 + 2 = 0. \tag{p₃}
$$
By the property (p₁) and the property (p₂), there exists an integer $N$ such that:
$$
a_1 > a_2 > \dots > a_{N-2} > a_{N-1} = 1.
$$
By the property (p₃), $a_{N-2}$ is the positive root of the equation
$$
x^2 - k a_{N-1} x + a_{N-1}^2 + 2 = 0 \Rightarrow x^2 - kx + 3 = 0, \text{ since } a_{N-1} = 1.
$$
Clearly, $a_{N-2}|3$ and by $a_{N-2} > a_{N-1} = 1$, we have $a_{N-2} = 3$. Thus, $k = 4$.
Since $a$ and $b$ satisfy the equation $a^2 + b^2 + 2 = 4ab$, similarly, we can prove that they are terms of the sequence defined by
$$
v_1 = v_2 = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad v_n = 4v_{n-1} - v_{n-2} \quad \forall n \ge 3.
$$
