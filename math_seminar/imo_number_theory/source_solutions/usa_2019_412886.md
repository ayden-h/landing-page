## Supplied solution 1

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the set of primes not exceeding $10^{100}$. For each $p \in \mathcal{P}$, let $e_p = \max_x \nu_p(f(x))$ and let $c_p = \underset{x}{\operatorname{argmax}} \nu_p(f(x))$.
We show that this is good enough to compute all values of $x$, by looking at the exponent at each individual prime.

**Claim** — For any $p \in \mathcal{P}$, we have
$$
\nu_p(f(x)) = \min(\nu_p(x - c_p), e_p).
$$
*Proof*. Note that for any $x$, we have
$$
\text{gcd}(f(c_p), f(x)) = \text{gcd}(f(c_p), x - c_p).
$$
We then take $\nu_p$ of both sides and recall $\nu_p(f(x)) \le \nu_p(f(c_p)) = e_p$; this implies the result. $\square$

This essentially determines $f$, and so now we just follow through. Choose $n$ and $m$ such that
$$
n = \prod_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p^{e_p} \\
 m \equiv -c_p \pmod{p^{e_p}} \quad \forall p \in \mathcal{P}
$$
the latter being possible by Chinese remainder theorem. Then, from the claim we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x) &= \prod_{p \in \mathcal{P}} p^{\nu_p(f(x))} = \prod_{p \nmid n} p^{\min(\nu_p(x-c_p), e_p)} \\
&= \prod_{p \mid n} p^{\min(\nu_p(x+m), \nu_p(n))} = \text{gcd}(x+m, n)
\end{aligned}
$$
for every $x \in \mathbb{Z}$, as desired.
