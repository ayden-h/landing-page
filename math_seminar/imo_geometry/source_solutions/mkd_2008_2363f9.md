## Supplied solution 1

We will prove that
$$
\frac{\sin \beta \cdot \sin \gamma}{1 - \cos \alpha \cdot \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma}
$$
belongs in $[-1, 1]$.

Since $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are acute angles we have $\cos \beta > 0$, $\cos \gamma > 0$, so $\cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma > 0$. Because $\cos \alpha \le 1$, we have that $\cos \alpha \cdot \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma \le \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma$, i.e. $-\cos \alpha \cdot \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma \ge -\cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma$.

From the sum identities, we have
$$
\sin \beta \cdot \sin \gamma + \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma = \cos(\beta - \gamma) \le 1.
$$
$$
\text{Then } 0 < \sin \beta \cdot \sin \gamma \le 1 - \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma \le 1 - \cos \alpha \cdot \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma.
$$
So
$$
0 < \frac{\sin \beta \cdot \sin \gamma}{1 - \cos \alpha \cdot \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma} \le 1.
$$
There is an angle $x$ such that
$$
\sin x = \frac{\sin \beta \cdot \sin \gamma}{1 - \cos \alpha \cdot \cos \beta \cdot \cos \gamma}.
$$
