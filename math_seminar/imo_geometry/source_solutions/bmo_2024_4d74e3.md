## Supplied solution 2

Applying cotangent rule for $\triangle PAB$, \triangle PBC$ and $\triangle PAC$ we have:
$$
\begin{aligned}
AB^2 &= PA^2 + PB^2 + \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}S_{PAB}, \\
BC^2 &= PB^2 + PC^2 + \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}S_{PBC}, \\
CA^2 &= PC^2 + PA^2 + \frac{4}{\sqrt{3}}S_{PCA} \\
\Rightarrow PA^2 + PB^2 + PC^2 &= \frac{AB^2 + BC^2 + CA^2}{2} - \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}S
\end{aligned}
$$
and the inequality is equivalent to
$$
AB \cdot AC + AC \cdot BC + BC \cdot AB \ge \frac{AB^2 + BC^2 + CA^2}{2} + 2\sqrt{3}S
$$
Using standard notations for a triangle we have
$$
a + b + c = 2p, \ ab + bc + ac = p^2 + r^2 + 4rR, \ S = pr.
$$
Therefore $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 2p^2 - 2r^2 - 8rR$ and we need to prove that
$$
\sqrt{3}p \le r + 4R.
$$
From Leibniz's inequality $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 \le 9R^2$ and Euler's inequality $r \le \frac{R}{2}$ we have
$$
3p^2 \le 3r^2 + 12Rr + \frac{27}{2}R^2 = (r + 4R)^2 + 2r^2 + 4Rr - \frac{5}{2}R^2 \le (r + 4R)^2,
$$
where the equation occurs if and only if $\triangle ABC$ is equilateral. $\square$
