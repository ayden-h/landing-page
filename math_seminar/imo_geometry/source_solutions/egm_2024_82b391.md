## Supplied solution 4

Let $\phi$ be the inversion with center $K$ and radius $KD$. Note that this inversion maps incircle of $ABC$ to itself and $K, X, Y$ are collinear, hence $\phi(X) = Y$. Also $\phi(D) = D$, so $\phi$ maps circle $XBD$ to circle $YDB'$, where $B' := \phi(B)$ is the point on $BC$ different from $B$ such that $\angle BXD = \angle DYB'$, hence $\phi(B) = C$. From $\phi(B) = C$ we get that $KD = KT$ as $KD^2 = KB \cdot KC = KT^2$ since $KD$ is the radius of inversion.
