## Supplied solution 1

Let $T$ be the center of mass of the triangle $\Delta ABC$ and $\overline{AA_1} = t_a$, $\overline{BB_1} = t_b$ are the two corresponding medians to the sides $BC$ and $AC$. From the right-angled triangle $\Delta BA_1T$, using that $\overline{A_1T} = \frac{1}{3}t_a$ and $\overline{TB} = \frac{2}{3}t_b$, we obtain
$$
\left(\frac{a}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{1}{3}t_a\right)^2 + \left(\frac{2}{3}t_b\right)^2.
$$
Similarly from the right-angled triangle $\Delta B_1AT$, using that $\overline{AT} = \frac{2}{3}t_a$ and $\overline{TB_1} = \frac{1}{3}t_b$, we have
$$
\left(\frac{b}{2}\right)^2 = \left(\frac{2}{3}t_a\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{3}t_b\right)^2.
$$
Summing the two equalities we get
$$
\frac{a^2 + b^2}{4} = \frac{5}{9}(t_a^2 + t_b^2)
$$
or
$$
t_a^2 + t_b^2 = \frac{9}{20}(a^2 + b^2).
$$
Finally from the right-angled triangle $\Delta ABT$ we obtain
$$
c^2 = \left(\frac{2}{3}t_a\right)^2 + \left(\frac{2}{3}t_b\right)^2 = \frac{4}{9}(t_a^2 + t_b^2) = \frac{a^2 + b^2}{5},
$$
from where it follows
$$
c = \sqrt{\frac{a^2 + b^2}{5}}.
$$
