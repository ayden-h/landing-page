## Supplied solution 1

Let $\angle BAC = \angle BCA = \alpha$, $\angle CBD = \angle CDB = \beta$, and $S$ the point of intersection of the lines $AB$ and $DC$.
If $AE=DE$, then $\frac{AE}{\sin(2\alpha + \beta)} = \frac{AB}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} = \frac{CD}{\sin(\alpha + \beta)} = \frac{DE}{\sin(\alpha + 2\beta)}$.

Now is obvious that
$$
\sin(2\alpha + \beta) = \sin(\alpha + 2\beta), \quad 0^{\circ} < 2\alpha + \beta < 180^{\circ}, \quad 0^{\circ} < \alpha + 2\beta < 180^{\circ},
$$
$\alpha \neq \beta$ (since $AC \neq BD$), and therefore
$$
2\alpha + \beta + \alpha + 2\beta = 180^{\circ} \text{ or } \alpha + \beta = 60^{\circ}.
$$
Hence we have: $\angle BAD + \angle ADC = 120^{\circ}$.

Conversely, if $\angle BAD + \angle ADC = 120^{\circ}$ we have
$$
\angle AEB = \angle BAE + \angle EDC \quad (1)
$$
$$
\angle AEB = \angle EAD + \angle EDA \quad (2)
$$
From (1) and (2) we have
$$
2(\angle AEB) = \angle BAD + \angle ADC = 120^{\circ}.
$$
Hence $\angle AEB = 60^{\circ} = \angle BSC$ and the quadrilateral $SBEC$ is inscribable. Therefore $\angle BSE = \angle BCE = \angle BAE$ and $AE=ES$.
Similarly we prove that $ES=ED$.
