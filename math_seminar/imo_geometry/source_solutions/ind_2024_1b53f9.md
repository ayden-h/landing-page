## Supplied solution 2

Observe that $\angle BOZ + \angle COY = 180^\circ$ hence the isogonal conjugate $Q$ of point $O$ in quadrilateral $BCYZ$ exists. Since $H$ and $O$ are isogonal conjugates in $ABC$, $Q=H$ follows.
In particular, $O$ is the centre of the circle passing through the reflections of $H$ in $BC, CY, YZ, ZB$; which coincides with the circumcircle of $ABC$. Thus reflection $P$ of $H$ in $YZ$ lies on $(ABC)$.
However, $\angle YHZ = \angle A$ as $\angle YOZ = 180^\circ - 2\angle A$ and $H, O$ are isogonal conjugates in $BCYZ$, hence $P$ lies on $(AYZ)$ as well.
Now $BZ = OB/\sin C$ hence $BZ \cdot BA = 2R^2 = CY \cdot CA$, where $R$ is the circumradius of $ABC$. Thus, $B$ and $C$ have equal powers in $(AYZ)$ or the circumcentre $O_1$ of triangle $AYZ$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $BC$, which passes through $O$.
Further, $O$ is the centre of spiral similarity sending $YZ$ to $QR$ so it suffices to show that it sends $O_1$ to $T$, where $T$ is the point of intersection of $Y$ and $Z$ tangents to $AYZ$.
Let $OO_1 \cap YZ = K$ and the line through $O$ parallel to $BC$ meet $QR$ at $S$. Now the same spiral similarity maps $K$ to $S$ so we just need to show that $OS/OK = OT/OO_1$. Both are equal to $\tan \angle (YZ, BC)$, so we are done.
To conclude, suffices to show $OD \perp YZ$ where $D$ is the foot of $A$-altitude which will allow $O, K, YZ \cap QR$ to be collinear. This follows as $HP \perp YZ$ as $H, O$ isogonal conjugates in $BCYZ$ hence
$$
\angle PAY = \angle PZY = \angle HZY = \angle OZB = \angle C.
$$
$\square$
