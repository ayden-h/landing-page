## Supplied solution 1

We first show that $\angle ADE = 90^\circ$. $\angle EB_1C_1 = \frac{1}{2}\angle A = \angle EC_1B_1$, hence we have $EB_1 = EC_1$. Similarly, we have $EB_1 = EA_1$, that is, $E$ is the circumcenter of $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$. On the other hand, $A$, $B$, $C$ are perpendicular feet of $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$ because, for example, $C_1$ lies on the bisector of $\angle ACB$ and $\angle B_1CC_1 = \angle B_1CA + \angle ACC_1 = (\frac{\pi}{2} - \frac{1}{2}\angle C) + (\frac{1}{2}\angle C) = \frac{\pi}{2}$. Hence the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$ is the nine-point circle of $\triangle A_1B_1C_1$, which means that $D$ is the midpoint of $B_1C_1$. Therefore, $\angle ADE = 90^\circ$.

Because $AE$ is a diameter of $\omega$ and $H$ is symmetric to $D$ with respect to $AE$, $HF$ is also tangent to $\omega$. Let $O_1$ be the center of $\omega$. Let $O_2$ be the circumcenter of $\triangle HGF$, then $O_2$ is the midpoint of $HF$. Since
$$
\angle HO_1F = \angle HO_1D = \angle HAD = \angle JAD,
$$
two triangles $\triangle HO_1F$ and $\triangle JAD$ are similar. For these triangles we have
$$
\angle HO_1O_2 = \frac{1}{2}\angle HO_1I = \angle HAI = \angle JAK,
$$
where $K = DJ \cap AI$. Since $HO_2 = O_2F$ in $\triangle HO_1F$, we have $JK = KD$ in $\triangle JAD$. $\square$
