## Supplied solution 1

Let $P$, $Q$ and $R$ be the circumcenters of the triangles $OBC$, $OCA$ and $OAB$, respectively, and let $r$ be their common circumradius.

Since $PB = PO = RB = RO = r$, the quadrilateral $BPOR$ is a rhombus and so $\vec{BR} = \vec{PO}$. Similarly, $\vec{CQ} = \vec{PO}$. So $\vec{BR} = \vec{CQ}$, and it follows that $BCQR$ is a parallelogram. Therefore $BC = QR$.

Similarly $CA = RP$ and $AB = PQ$ follows, and we obtain $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$. Since the points $P$, $Q$ and $R$ are on the circle with center $O$ and radius $r$, the circumradius of triangle $PQR$ is $r$. So $r$ is equal to the circumradius of triangle $ABC$.

By cosine formula, $\cos \angle B = \frac{AB^2 + BC^2 - CA^2}{2AB \cdot BC} = \frac{1}{2}$. By sine formula, $r = \frac{CA}{2 \sin \angle B} = \frac{7}{\sqrt{3}}$.
