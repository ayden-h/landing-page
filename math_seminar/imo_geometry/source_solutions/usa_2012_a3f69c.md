## Supplied solution 2

We maintain the notations of the first solution. The result clearly follows from the following two lemmas.

**Lemma 1.** The sum of the perimeters of triangles $DEF$ and $PQR$ is at least the perimeter of triangle $ABC$.

*Proof.* By symmetry, it suffices to show that $DE + PQ \ge AB$. Let $M$ and $N$ be the midpoints of segments $CA$ and $CB$, respectively. We want to show that $DE + PQ \ge AB = 2MN$. As in the first solution, we have $CE = AQ$ and $CD = BP$ so that $M$ and $N$ are midpoints of segments $PD$ and $QE$, respectively. Computing using vectors, we obtain
$$
\begin{aligned}
\overrightarrow{MN} &= \overrightarrow{MC} + \overrightarrow{CN} = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{QC} + \overrightarrow{EC}) + \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{CP} + \overrightarrow{CD}) \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{QC} + \overrightarrow{CP}) + \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{EC} + \overrightarrow{CD}) = \frac{1}{2}(\overrightarrow{QP} + \overrightarrow{ED}).
\end{aligned}
$$
By the triangle inequality, we have $MN \le \frac{1}{2}(QP + ED)$, which implies
$$
PQ + DE \ge 2MN = AB. \quad \square
$$

**Lemma 2.** The perimeter of triangle $ABC$ is at least twice that of triangle $PQR$.

*Proof.* Note that $PQ = 2CP \sin \frac{C}{2}$, $QR = 2AQ \sin \frac{A}{2}$, and $RP = 2BR \sin \frac{B}{2}$. The perimeter of triangle $PQR$ is equal to
$$
S_{PQR} = (b+c-a) \sin \frac{A}{2} + (c+a-b) \sin \frac{B}{2} + (a+b-c) \sin \frac{C}{2}.
$$
By symmetry, we may assume that $a \le b \le c$. This yields the orderings
$$
A \le B \le C, \quad b+c-a \ge c+a-b \ge a+b-c, \quad \text{and} \quad \sin \frac{A}{2} \le \sin \frac{B}{2} \le \sin \frac{C}{2}.
$$
By Chebyshev's inequality, we have
$$
S_{PQR} \le \frac{1}{3} \left( (b+c-a) + (c+a-b) + (a+b-c) \right) \left( \sin \frac{A}{2} + \sin \frac{B}{2} + \sin \frac{C}{2} \right),
$$
so it suffices to show that
$$
\sin \frac{A}{2} + \sin \frac{B}{2} + \sin \frac{C}{2} \le \frac{3}{2}. \qquad (28)
$$
But (28) follows from Jensen's inequality for $y = \sin x$ (which is concave for $0 \le x \le \frac{\pi}{2}$). $\square$
