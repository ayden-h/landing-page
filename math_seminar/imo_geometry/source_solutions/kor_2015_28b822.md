## Supplied solution 1

Let $R$ be the center of the circle $\omega$, and $E$ be the intersecting points of $BH$ and $AC$. Then we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
\angle RBD &= 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2}\angle DRB && \text{since } RB = RD \\
&= 90^\circ - \angle DHB \\
&= 90^\circ - \angle PHE \\
&= \angle EPH \\
&= 180^\circ - \angle APD
\end{aligned}
\qquad (8)
$$
On the other hand, if we let $M$ be the midpoint of $AD$ then $M$, $Q$, and $R$ are collinear, and it holds that
$$
\begin{aligned}
\angle MQD &= \frac{1}{2}\angle AQD \\
&= \frac{1}{2}(360^\circ - 2\angle APD) && \text{since } A \text{ is a circumcenter} \\
&= 180^\circ - \angle APD \\
&= \angle RBD && \text{by (8)}
\end{aligned}
$$
Thus $R$ lies on the circumscribed circle of $\triangle BDQ$.
