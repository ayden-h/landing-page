## Supplied solution 1

We will show that the player B can finish the game no matter how A plays.
Suppose we represent by $(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N)$ the list of numbers written into the $N$ boxes from left to right. Let also $x_0 = 0$. For this list we call the index $i$ $(1 \le i \le N-1)$ the **index of completion** if it is the smallest index which satisfies $x_i > x_{i+1}$. If such an $i$ does not exist, we let the index of completion be $N$. If at some stage of the game the index of completion is $N$, then the game ends at that stage.
Suppose at some stage the index of completion $j$ is less than $N$. Then, no matter what non-negative integer $a$ the player A chooses, the player B can use the following strategy (†) to make sure one of the following two possibilities will occur:
(i) The index of completion increases.
(ii) The index of completion remains the same, but the sum of all the numbers written in the boxes decreases at least by 1.

Strategy (†):
* If $x_j \le a$, replace $x_{j+1}$ by $a$. Then the index of completion increases to at least $j+1$.
* If $x_j > a$, there exists a unique $i$ with $1 \le i \le j$ for which $x_{i-1} \le a < x_i$. So, replace $x_i$ for this $i$ by $a$. Then the index of completion remains at $j$ and the sum of all the numbers written in the boxes decreases at least by 1.

Since all the integers written in the boxes are non-negative at any stage, it is not possible for the sum of these numbers to keep on decreasing at least by 1 indefinitely while the index of completion is remaining the same. So, the index of completion will keep on increasing, and after a finite number of repetitions the index of completion reaches $N$ and the game will be over and the player B will be the winner.
