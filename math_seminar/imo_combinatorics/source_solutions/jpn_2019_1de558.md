## Supplied solution 1

It is clear that whether the number inserted at every turn matters for the point(s) earned or not depends only on the remainder obtained when that number is divided by $n$. Therefore, we may assume that every integer $k$ satisfying $0 \le k \le n - 1$ will be inserted into squares exactly $n$ times.

Let us show that the value we seek as the answer to the problem is $n(n+1)$. We first exhibit a method of inserting numbers for which the total number of points earned at the end of the game is exactly $n(n+1)$. This method tells us to insert into the square located at the $i$-th row from the top and $j$-th column from the left the number obtained as the remainder when $i+j$ is divided by $n$. If $0$ is inserted $n$ times, $1$ is inserted $n$ times, $n-1$ is inserted $n$ times, $2$ is inserted $n$ times, $n-2$ is inserted $n$ times, ..., then, whenever $0, n-1, n-2, \dots, \frac{n+1}{2}$ are inserted, $2$ points are earned so that the total of $n(n+1)$ points will be earned at the end.

Next, let us show that the total number of points earned by the end of the game can be at most $n(n+1)$. Let $A_0 = B_0 = 0$. For any $i$ satisfying $1 \le i \le n^2$, let $A_i = A_{i-1} + 1$ if the sum of the numbers written into the squares lying on the same row of the grid as the square into which a number is inserted at the $i$-th turn (which is included in the sum) is a multiple of $n$, and $A_i = A_{i-1}$ otherwise. We define $B_i$'s for columns in the same way. Then, the total sum of the points earned at the end of the game is given by $A_{n^2} + B_{n^2}$.

Let us consider the maximum value that $A_{n^2}$ can take. For every $i$ satisfying $0 \le i \le n^2$, let us denote by $C_i$ the number of columns for which the column-sum of the numbers inserted into the squares is not a multiple of $n$ right after the end of $i$-th turn. We also let $C_0 = 0$. Then, we see that $A_i + \frac{1}{2}C_i$ increases, in comparison with $A_{i-1} + \frac{1}{2}C_{i-1}$ at most by $1$ if $0$ is the number inserted at the $i$-th turn, and at most by $\frac{1}{2}$ otherwise. Since $0$ is inserted $n$ times and non-zero $n$ is inserted $n^2 - n = n(n - 1)$ times, we see that
$$
A_{n^2} + \frac{1}{2}C_{n^2} \le n + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}
$$
must hold. Since $C_{n^2}$ is a non-negative integer, we conclude that $A_{n^2} \le \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$ must be satisfied. By the same argument we obtain $B_{n^2} \le \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$. Therefore, the maximum of the total points we obtain at the end of the game is $n(n+1)$.
