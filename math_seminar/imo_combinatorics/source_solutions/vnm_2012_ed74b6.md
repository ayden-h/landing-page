## Supplied solution 1

We denote a boy by the letter $b$ and a girl by the letter $g$. We can check that the total number of the sweets is exactly $\frac{1}{3}n(n^2 - 1)$ if the line can be partitioned into $n$ consecutive pairs of one boy and one girl. This arrangement will be called the optimal arrangement.

Any other arrangement will be in one of the following forms
$$
\begin{array}{l} (bg)(bg)(gb) \dots (..) \underbrace{gg \dots g}_{t \ge 2} b \dots, \text{ or} \\ (bg)(bg)(gb) \dots (..) \underbrace{bb \dots b}_{t \ge 2} g \dots \end{array} \qquad (7)
$$
Then, it's easily to check that, by moving one girl or one boy as shown in the arrangements (8), the number of sweets is increased. Therefore, after a finite number of moves, we will get the optimal arrangement with exact

$\frac{1}{3}n(n^2 - 1)$ sweets. This concludes the proof.
$$
(bg)(bg)(gb) \dots (..) \underbrace{gg \dots g}_{t-1} bg \dots, \text{ and} \\ (bg)(bg)(gb) \dots (..) \underbrace{bb \dots b}_{t-1} gb \dots \qquad (8)
$$
