## Supplied solution 1

Notice that the initially given numbers are all odd, and that the number that replaces some three of them is again an odd one, so after the replacement we are left again with a set of odd numbers. The same reasoning applies to every replacement, thus all numbers on blackboard are always odd ones. So each one of the two numbers remaining on the blackboard at the end is odd, and the sum of their squares is even, which of course cannot equal any of the odd numbers $1^2, 3^2, \dots, (2n-1)^2$ written initially on the blackboard. $\square$
